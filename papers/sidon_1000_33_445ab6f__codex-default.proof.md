Translation preserves pair-sum collisions: for any \(x,y,u,v\),
$$
(x+1)+(y+1)=(u+1)+(v+1)\iff x+y=u+v.
$$
So it is enough to prove that \(T\) is Sidon.

I will prove the stronger statement that all positive differences \(t_j-t_i\) \((i<j)\) are distinct, i.e. that \(T\) is a Golomb ruler. This stronger property implies the Sidon property: if
$$
t_i+t_j=t_k+t_\ell\qquad (i<j,\ k<\ell)
$$
and \(\{i,j\}\neq\{k,\ell\}\), then after relabeling we may assume \(t_i<t_k\). Hence
$$
t_\ell-t_j=t_k-t_i>0.
$$
So two positive differences coincide. If all positive differences are distinct, this is impossible. Note that this uses only the forward implication “Golomb ruler \(\Rightarrow\) Sidon”; it avoids the subtle converse issue for the weak Sidon notion here, where repeated-term sums \(2y\) are irrelevant because only sums with \(a<b\) are forbidden.
