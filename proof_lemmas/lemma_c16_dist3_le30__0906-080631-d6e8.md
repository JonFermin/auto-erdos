# Lemma `c16_dist3_le30` (proved — the dist-3 invariant of the arc-exchange program holds for $24 \le n \le 30$)

**Statement.** Let $G$ be a connected cubic $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free graph
on $n$ vertices with $24 \le n \le 30$, let $C$ be a chordless
$16$-cycle of $G$, and let $v \notin V(C)$ be a $0$-spoke outside
vertex of $C$ (no neighbour of $v$ lies on $C$). Then
$\mathrm{dist}(v, C) \le 3$.

This is invariant (dist-3) of the R73 refinement of
`arc_exchange_witness`, now proved on all of $24 \le n \le 30$ — and
the bound is best possible in $n$: distance $4$ actually occurs at
$n = 32$ (sharpness witness in CHECK 4 below), so the invariant does
NOT extend and the statement's range is final. It is the first proved
rung of the arc-exchange program: at $n \le 30$ it bounds the branch
path from $v$ to $C$ by length $3$, which is what pins the
exchange-menu $(a, b, c)$ triples to a finite list.

**Notation.** $T$ = the set of *touched* outside vertices (those
receiving at least one spoke of $C$); $Z$ = the remaining outside
vertices ($0$-spoke). $B(u, r)$ = the ball of radius $r$ around $u$
in $G$. By `chordless_c16_ear_geometry`(a), $C$ has exactly $16$
spokes, all ending in $T$, so $|V(G)| = 16 + |T| + |Z|$.

## Part 1 — the radius-2 ball bound in a cubic $C_4$-free graph

**Claim.** For every vertex $u$ of a cubic $C_4$-free simple graph:
$|B(u, 2)| = 8$ if $u$ lies on a triangle, and $|B(u, 2)| = 10$
otherwise. In particular $|B(u, 2)| \ge 8$ always.

*Proof.* Let $N(u) = \{p, q, r\}$ (three distinct neighbours; $G$ is
cubic and simple). $N(u)$ spans at most one edge: if $pq, qr \in E$
then $u\,p\,q\,r\,u$ (edges $up$, $pq$, $qr$, $ru$, all present, four
distinct vertices) is a $C_4$; excluded.

*Case A ($N(u)$ edge-free — $u$ on no triangle).* Each of $p, q, r$
has two neighbours outside $\{u\} \cup N(u)$ (it cannot be adjacent
to another member of $N(u)$ by edge-freeness, nor to $u$ twice). No
two of these six second-neighbour slots coincide: a common neighbour
$w \ne u$ of $p$ and $q$ closes the $C_4$ $u\,p\,w\,q\,u$; the two
slots of a single parent are distinct by simplicity. So
$|B(u, 2)| = 1 + 3 + 6 = 10$.

*Case B ($pq \in E$ — $u$ on the triangle $u p q$).* $p$ and $q$
each have exactly one neighbour off $\{u, p, q\}$ (call them $p',
q'$); $r$ has two ($r_1, r_2$; $r$ is not adjacent to $p$ or $q$ —
that would be a second edge in $N(u)$). $p' \ne q'$ (a common
neighbour of $p, q$ beside $u$ gives the $C_4$ $u\,p\,p'\,q\,u$);
$r_i \notin \{p', q'\}$ ($r_i = p'$ means $r_i$ is adjacent to both
$r$ and $p$, closing the $C_4$ $u\,r\,r_i\,p\,u$); $r_1 \ne r_2$ by
simplicity. So $|B(u, 2)| = 1 + 3 + 4 = 8$. $\square$

## Part 2 — $\mathrm{dist}(v, C) \ge 4$ forces $n \ge 30$

Suppose $\mathrm{dist}(v, C) \ge 4$. Every $u \in B(v, 2)$ has
$\mathrm{dist}(u, C) \ge 4 - 2 = 2$, so $u \notin V(C)$ and $u
\notin T$ ($T$ sits at distance $1$): the whole ball lies in $Z$,
giving $|Z| \ge |B(v, 2)| \ge 8$ by Part 1. Separately, the $16$
spokes end in $T$ and each outside vertex has degree $3$, so $|T|
\ge \lceil 16/3 \rceil = 6$. Hence
$n = 16 + |T| + |Z| \ge 16 + 6 + 8 = 30$. In particular
$\mathrm{dist}(v, C) \le 3$ for all $n \le 28$ — with room to spare
($n = 29$ is odd, hence not cubic-realizable anyway). $\square$

## Part 3 — the $n = 30$ case: the forced "plug" does not exist

Suppose $n = 30$ and $\mathrm{dist}(v, C) \ge 4$. Part 2's chain is
tight, so every inequality is an equality:

1. $|T| = 6$, $|Z| = 8$, and $Z = B(v, 2)$ exactly (the ball has
   $\ge 8$ vertices inside $Z$ and $|Z| = 8$). By Part 1's equality
   case, $v$ lies on a triangle of $G[Z]$.
2. The spoke multiset on $T$ sums to $16$ with entries $\le 3$:
   profile $(3,3,3,3,3,1)$ or $(3,3,3,3,2,2)$. The number of
   *non-spoke* edge-endpoints on $T$ is $\sum_{t}(3 - s_t) = 18 -
   16 = 2$.
3. Those $2$ endpoints cannot form an internal $T$–$T$ edge: then no
   edge would leave $Z$ at all ($Z$-vertices are $0$-spoke, $T$-edges
   to $C$ are spokes), disconnecting $Z \ne \emptyset$ from $C$ —
   $G$ is connected. They cannot go to $C$ (every $C$-vertex's third
   edge is its spoke, already counted). So there are EXACTLY $2$
   edges between $Z$ and $T$, and $G[Z]$ has degree sequence
   $(3^6, 2^2)$ (two $Z$-endpoints distinct) or $(3^7, 1)$ (both on
   one vertex).
4. Every $z \in Z$ satisfies $\mathrm{dist}_{G[Z]}(v, z) \le 2$: a
   $v$–$z$ path of length $\le 2$ cannot pass through $T \cup V(C)$
   (an internal vertex $t \in T$ gives $\mathrm{dist}(v, C) \le
   \mathrm{dist}(v,t) + 1 \le 2$), so it lives in $G[Z]$.
5. Each $Z$-vertex $z_i$ carrying a $Z$–$T$ edge ("deficit" vertex)
   has $\mathrm{dist}_{G[Z]}(v, z_i) = 2$ exactly: $z_i = v$ gives
   $\mathrm{dist}(v, C) \le 2$ and $z_i \in N(v)$ gives
   $\mathrm{dist}(v, C) \le 3$, both contradicting
   $\mathrm{dist}(v, C) \ge 4$; and $\le 2$ by item 4.
6. $G[Z]$ is an induced subgraph of $G$, hence $C_4$-free and
   $C_8$-free.

So $G[Z]$ would be a graph on $8$ vertices with degree sequence
$(3^6, 2^2)$ or $(3^7, 1)$, $C_4$-free, $C_8$-free, all vertices
within distance $2$ of $v$, deficit vertices at distance exactly
$2$. Exhaustive enumeration (vertex $0 = v$; per-vertex neighbour
completion over all $\binom{7}{2} = 21$ deficit pairs and $7$
deficit singletons; each labeled graph generated exactly once;
exact $C_4$/$C_8$/distance tests):

| stage | count |
|---|---|
| labeled graphs meeting a degree sequence | 129,584 |
| ... and $C_4$-free | 7,560 (uniformly $360$ per $(3^6,2^2)$ deficit pair; the $(3^7,1)$ sequence admits NO $C_4$-free graph) |
| ... and radius-$2$ from $v$ with deficits at distance $2$ | 2,520 |
| ... and $C_8$-free | **0** |

Every one of the $2{,}520$ surviving graphs contains an $8$-cycle.
No valid plug exists, so $\mathrm{dist}(v, C) \ge 4$ is impossible
at $n = 30$. $\square$

**Enumerator validation** (R68 discipline — never trust a fresh
enumerator): the identical per-vertex completion DFS with all
targets $3$ and no filters reproduces the known count of labeled
cubic graphs on $8$ vertices, $19{,}355$; and the incremental
$C_4$-prune (skip a neighbour set containing $a, b$ that already
share a common neighbour — the new vertex would be a second one) is
validated differentially against filter-at-leaf on the
$(3^6, 2^2)$ sequence: $360 = 360$. Both are CHECK 1 below; the
full Part-3 enumeration with all four counts is CHECK 2.

**The bound is SHARP: distance $4$ is realized at $n = 32$.** Part 2
leaves room exactly at $n = 32$ ($|T| + |Z| = 16$, $(|T|, |Z|) \in
\{(6,10), (7,9), (8,8)\}$), and reality fills it: the R74 walk found
$37$ pairs at distance exactly $4$ (never $5$) among $201{,}612$
$n = 32$ pairs, and CHECK 4 below pins a reproducible one — a class
member on $32$ vertices with a (chordless $C_{16}$, $0$-spoke $v$)
pair at $\mathrm{dist}(v, C) = 4$ on which the arc-exchange witness
STILL exists ($163$ edge-sharing chorded witnesses through $v$, max
share $8$, min off-$C$ $7$). So this lemma cannot extend to
$n = 32$, and the $n = 32$ branch of `arc_exchange_witness` must
work with $\mathrm{dist} \le 4$ (observed; a proof of THAT bound
via the same forcing — $\mathrm{dist} \ge 5$ pushes $B(v, 3)$ into
$Z$ — is the natural companion target).
