# Lemma `c16_dist4_n32` (proved — at $n = 32$ the branch-vertex distance is at most $4$, and this is sharp)

**Statement.** Let $G$ be a connected cubic $\{C_4, C_8\}$-free graph
on $32$ vertices, $C$ a chordless $16$-cycle of $G$, and
$v \notin V(C)$ a $0$-spoke outside vertex of $C$. Then
$\mathrm{dist}(v, C) \le 4$. The bound is sharp: distance $4$ occurs
(`c16_dist3_le30` CHECK 4 exhibits a class member realizing it).

**Corollary (branch-distance theorem, $24 \le n \le 32$).** Combining
with `c16_dist3_le30`: in every class member on $24 \le n \le 32$
vertices, every $0$-spoke outside vertex of every chordless $C_{16}$
is at distance $\le 3$ (for $n \le 30$) resp. $\le 4$ (for $n = 32$)
from the cycle, and both bounds are attained. (This bounds the
SHORTEST route from $v$ to $C$; bounding the length menu of the
exchange's two disjoint routes — which need not be shortest — is the
remaining step between this theorem and the $(a, b, c)$ menu lemma,
and is where the off-$C$ budget $|Z| \le 16 - |T|$ enters.)

**Notation** as in `c16_dist3_le30`: $T$ = touched outside vertices
(with $\ge 1$ spoke), $Z$ = $0$-spoke outside vertices, deficit
vertex = a $Z$-vertex with at least one edge into $T$. At $n = 32$:
$|T| + |Z| = 16$, and `chordless_c16_ear_geometry`(a) gives $16$
spokes ending in $T$, so $|T| \ge \lceil 16/3 \rceil = 6$. Every
edge of a $Z$-vertex goes to $Z$ or $T$ ($C$-vertices carry two
cycle edges plus their spoke — full). The number of non-spoke
edge-endpoints on $T$ is $\sigma = 3|T| - 16$; with $t$ internal
$T$–$T$ edges, $e_{ZT} = \sigma - 2t$, and $e_{ZT} \ge 1$ always
($e_{ZT} = 0$ disconnects $Z \ne \emptyset$ from $C$).

**Proof.** Suppose $\mathrm{dist}(v, C) \ge 5$.

*Step 1 — deficit vertices avoid $B(v, 2)$.* A deficit vertex sits
at distance exactly $2$ from $C$ (one hop to $T$, one to $C$), while
every $u \in B(v, 2)$ has $\mathrm{dist}(u, C) \ge 5 - 2 = 3$. So
$B(v, 2) \subseteq Z$, $|Z| \ge |B(v, 2)| + d$ where $d \ge 1$ is
the number of deficit vertices, and $|B(v, 2)| \in \{8, 10\}$ by the
ball bound (`c16_dist3_le30` Part 1; $8$ iff $v$ on a triangle).

*Step 2 — $|T| = 8$ is impossible.* $|Z| = 8 < 8 + d$. $\square$

*Step 3 — $|T| = 7$.* $|Z| = 9$ forces $|B(v, 2)| = 8$ ($10 + 1 >
9$), $d = 1$, and $Z = B(v, 2) \sqcup \{z^*\}$ with $z^*$ the sole
deficit vertex absorbing all $e_{ZT}$ edges, so $e_{ZT} \le 3$.
$\sigma = 5$ gives $e_{ZT} = 5 - 2t \in \{1, 3\}$ ($t \in \{2, 1\}$;
$t = 0$ would need $e_{ZT} = 5 > 3$), hence $z^*$ has $G[Z]$-degree
$3 - e_{ZT} \in \{0, 2\}$.

*Step 4 — $|T| = 6$.* $\sigma = 2$; $t = 1$ gives $e_{ZT} = 0$
(disconnection), so $t = 0$, $e_{ZT} = 2$. $|Z| = 10$ forces
$|B(v, 2)| = 8$ ($10 + d > 10$ otherwise), so $Z = B(v, 2) \sqcup
\{e_1, e_2\}$ and the two $Z$–$T$ edges land on $\{e_1, e_2\}$:
either one each ($G[Z]$-degrees $2, 2$) or both on one ($G[Z]$-degrees
$1, 3$ — a double edge to one $t$ is not simple, so the two edges go
to distinct $T$-vertices, which does not matter below).

*Step 5 — the ball is rigid and the completion space is tiny.* In
every surviving case $v$ lies on a triangle and $B(v, 2) =
\{v, a, b, x, a', b', x_1, x_2\}$ with edge set exactly
$\{va, vb, vx, ab, aa', bb', xx_1, xx_2\}$ inside
$\{v, a, b, x\} \times B(v,2)$: the degrees of $v, a, b, x$ are
already full ($v$: $a, b, x$; $a$: $v, b, a'$; $b$: $v, a, b'$;
$x$: $v, x_1, x_2$), so every further $G[Z]$-edge joins two vertices
of $\{a', b', x_1, x_2\} \cup \{$extras$\}$. The boundary vertices
$a', b', x_1, x_2$ are at distance $2$ from $v$, hence at distance
$\ge 3$ from $C$, hence NOT deficit: each needs exactly $2$ more
$G[Z]$-edges. The extras need their forced degrees from Steps 3–4,
and are non-adjacent to $v, a, b, x$ (full). The pair $a'b'$ is
excluded ($a\,a'\,b'\,b\,a$ would be a $C_4$; the exact filter
would catch it anyway).

*Step 6 — enumeration.* All degree-feasible completions in the four
cases, each tested exactly for $C_4$- and $C_8$-freeness of $G[Z]$
(both inherited from $G$, so necessary):

| case | degree-feasible completions | $C_4$+$C_8$-free |
|---|---|---|
| $|T|{=}7$, $e_{ZT}{=}3$ ($z^*$ deg $0$) | 1 | 0 |
| $|T|{=}7$, $e_{ZT}{=}1$ ($z^*$ deg $2$) | 6 | 0 |
| $|T|{=}6$, split $(1,1)$ (degs $2,2$) | 42 | 0 |
| $|T|{=}6$, split $(2,0)$ (degs $1,3$) | 22 | 0 |

Zero survivors (CHECK 1 re-runs the whole enumeration). So
$\mathrm{dist}(v, C) \ge 5$ is impossible at $n = 32$. $\square$

**Remark on method.** Only *necessary* conditions on $G[Z]$ were
imposed — no T-side or spoke-profile analysis was needed. The same
forcing pattern closed dist-4 at $n = 30$ (`c16_dist3_le30` Part 3)
and dist-5 here; each time the $C_8$-freeness of the forced plug is
what fails last. The 71 completions here are few because the
radius-2 ball is combinatorially rigid (Part 1's equality analysis
fixes 8 of the $\le 15$ edges).
